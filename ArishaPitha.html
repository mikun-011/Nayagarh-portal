Arisha Pitha

Rice, jaggery and patience

About

Arisha Pitha (also written Arisa Pitha) is a deep-fried round sweet of rice flour and jaggery, one of the oldest and best-loved pithas of Odisha. Crisp at the edges and dense and chewy inside, it is shaped by hand on a banana leaf and fried until it turns a glossy dark golden brown — just like the ones in the photograph.

The dough is made by grinding soaked rice into flour and kneading it with boiled jaggery syrup and cardamom. Traditional cooks let the dough rest for several days so that the flavour deepens before frying, and sesame seeds are often added. After frying, each pitha is pressed gently to squeeze out the extra oil.

Cousins of the Arisha Pitha are found across southern India: adhirasam in Tamil Nadu, ariselu in Telugu-speaking regions, kajjaya in Karnataka and anarasa in Maharashtra. Its long history is traced to medieval royal and temple kitchens. In Odisha it is a festive sweet, made at home when families celebrate.

How It Is Made

  1. Soak rice, dry it in the shade and grind it to a fine, slightly moist flour.
  2. Boil jaggery with water to a soft-ball syrup.
  3. Mix the syrup, rice flour and cardamom into a thick dough and let it rest for a few days.
  4. Flatten small balls on an oiled banana leaf, add sesame and deep fry until golden.
  5. Press out the excess oil with a flat-bottomed bowl and let it cool.

Where to Try It

Look for Arisha Pitha at local sweet shops and stalls, especially in festival season, and ask for it fresh — the crust is at its best on the day it is fried.
