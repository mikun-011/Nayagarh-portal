Chhena Poda

Odisha’s very own baked cheesecake

About

OriginDaspalla, Nayagarh, 1940s
Made fromChhena, sugar, semolina, cardamom
Famous atItamati and Machhipada
Celebrated on11 April (Chhenapoda Dibasa)

Chhena Poda — “burnt cheese” in Odia — is Nayagarh’s gift to Odisha: fresh chhena kneaded with sugar and semolina, flavoured with cardamom, studded with cashews and raisins, and slow-roasted until the top turns dark, caramel brown and smoky while the inside stays soft and moist.

The sweet is traditionally credited to Sudarshan Sahoo, born on 11 April 1931 in Satapatna village of Daspalla block, who is said to have left sweetened chhena in a warm oven overnight in the 1940s and found it baked to perfection the next morning. Family accounts also credit his father, Bidyadhar Sahoo. Odisha now observes Chhenapoda Dibasa on 11 April, and Daspalla marks 31 December in memory of Bidyadhar. India’s first Prime Minister, Jawaharlal Nehru, is said to have enjoyed it.

Today the best Chhena Poda is said to come from Itamati and Machhipada. Around 110 families in Machhipada village bake it, and transporters carry it from Nayagarh to the rest of Odisha and beyond.

How It Is Made

  1. Knead fresh chhena with sugar, a little semolina, ghee and cardamom.
  2. Spread the mix in a shallow pan lined with a leaf (sal leaf in the traditional story) and top with cashews and raisins.
  3. Roast slowly over a low fire or charcoal, with heat above and below.
  4. Cool, then cut. The crust should be dark and caramelised, the centre soft.

GI Tag Status

An application for a Geographical Indication tag for Nayagarh Chhenapoda (application no. 1067) was filed on 30 March 2023 by ORMAS Nayagarh. It is still under examination at the GI registry, so the tag has not yet been granted.

Tasting Tips

Eat it fresh, at room temperature or slightly warm. Buy from sweet shops in Nayagarh town, Itamati or Daspalla, and ask for a piece with plenty of crust — that is where the caramel flavour lives.
