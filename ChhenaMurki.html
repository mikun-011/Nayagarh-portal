Chhena Murki

Soft inside, crystal crunch outside

About

Chhena Murki is a sweet of soft cubes of fresh chhena (cottage cheese) coated in a thin, crunchy shell of crystallised sugar. The word murki describes this sugary coating, which gives the sweet its crisp bite over a tender centre.

Cardamom is the traditional flavouring, and the cubes are often finished with sliced almonds or a sheet of edible silver leaf. It is best served at room temperature, after a meal or with tea.

Chhena Murki is a popular festival sweet of eastern India, often served at Durga Puja, Diwali, Holi and Bhai Dooj alongside rasgulla and sandesh. It is different from Odisha’s chhena gaja and chhena khurma, which are fried and caramelised.

How It Is Made

  1. Cut fresh chhena into even 2.5–3 cm cubes and let them air-dry for 10–15 minutes.
  2. Boil sugar and water to a one-string syrup.
  3. Add the cubes and cook gently for 5–6 minutes until the syrup foams.
  4. Add cardamom, then stir steadily until the sugar crystallises and coats each cube — 10 to 20 minutes.
  5. Separate the cubes, cool them and shake off loose sugar.
