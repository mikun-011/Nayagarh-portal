Ladoo Baba Temple

Ladukeshwar · Sarankul

About

DeityLord Shiva as Ladukeshwar
PlaceSarankul, Nayagarh district
DistanceAbout 13–14 km from Nayagarh town
Big dayMaha Shivaratri (Jagara Yatra)

Ladoo Baba Temple, also called the Ladukeshwar Temple, stands in the small town of Sarankul (Sharanakula), about 13–14 km from Nayagarh town. It is dedicated to Lord Shiva, worshipped here as Ladukeshwar and lovingly known to devotees as Ladoo Baba, and it is counted among the most important Shaiva pilgrimage sites of southern Odisha.

The temple sits at the foot of Bhandar Hill in a green, hilly setting. Local tradition links the hill with the demon Banasura, who is said to have stored his weapons there. The shrine itself is described as medieval, and its style blends Kalinga temple architecture with Dravidian touches. Brightly painted smaller shrines surround the main sanctum, a large Nandi faces the entrance, and a pond lies beside the complex.

A local account says the temple was raised by a king of Nayagarh who gave it his own name, Ladukeshwar. Its exact date is not firmly documented — sources give anything from the 9th to the 15th century.

Worship & Offerings

Arati is performed every morning and evening. The deity is offered 66 different bhogs, and a special offering called Mandua Panaa is believed by devotees to bring relief to troubled minds.

The temple’s prasad, called Abadha, is sold at modest prices. Outside food is not allowed inside the premises.

Maha Shivaratri

Maha Shivaratri, popularly called Jagara Yatra, falls on Falguna Shukla Chaturdashi. Pilgrims fill the town and keep an all-night vigil of worship. Rituals and festivals here are said to resemble those of the Lingaraj Temple in Bhubaneswar.

Visiting

Reported temple timings are 5:30 am to 12:30 pm and 6:00 pm to 8:00 pm — please confirm locally. Sarankul is reached by the state highway from Bhubaneswar towards Odagaon; regular buses run from Bhubaneswar and the nearest railhead is Nayagarh Town station.

Nearby you can see Jhadeswari, a scenic spot in the Bhandar valley, and the Salia waterfall.
