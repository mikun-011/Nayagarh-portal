Kantilo Temple (Nilamadhab)

Where the Mahanadi meets the Kuanria and Kusumi

About

DeityLord Nilamadhab (Vishnu)
PlaceKantilo, right bank of the Mahanadi
DistanceAbout 33 km from Nayagarh, 82 km from Bhubaneswar
Big dayMagha Saptami

Kantilo, a quiet village on the right bank of the Mahanadi, is home to the Nilamadhab Temple, a Vishnu shrine ringed by forest and low hills. It lies about 33 km from Nayagarh town and 82 km from Bhubaneswar, close to the point where the Mahanadi is joined by the Kuanria and Kusumi rivers — a confluence local people call a triveni.

Nilamadhab holds a special place in the story of Lord Jagannath. According to the Puranas, the deity was first worshipped as a blue sapphire (Indranila) image by the Sabara chief Biswabasu, who called him Kitung; the wooden form of Jagannath came much later. In Puri, a shrine of Nilamadhava stands beside the Lakshmi temple inside the Jagannath Temple — a reminder of this older origin.

The temple is said to resemble the Jagannath temple of Puri, and worship is believed to have begun in a small cave in the Brahmadri hills on the river bank. Visitors speak of a permanent flow of holy water from the deity’s feet and of a nearby shrine of Lord Siddheswar.

Festivals

Magha Saptami (Ratha Saptami) is the main festival. A fair is held on the Ekadashi of Magha, and on Pausa Purnima the deity is decorated with gold ornaments.

Getting There

Take a train or bus to Bhubaneswar and then a regular bus towards Kantilo, or reach Nayagarh Town station (the nearest railhead) and continue by road. The nearest airport is Bhubaneswar.
